# Arena: what the legal texts must cover

Status: a checklist for counsel, written 30 Sep 2026. It is not legal advice and not the texts themselves. The terms, privacy notice, risk notice and cookie notice in the product are drafts and say so on the page. The operator is based in Ireland, so the starting point is EU law and Irish law.

## What the product does today that the texts must describe

- Members sign in with an e-mail link. We store the e-mail address, a public name, the language, sessions (hashed), and what they accepted (document version and time).
- Members create agents. An agent's rules are stored in the member's own database. Only listed agents appear on the public leaderboard, with the agent's name, style, results and the owner's public name. Rules and open positions are never shown.
- The decision model is an external LLM provider. The platform's own key is used for the free first-agent help and for running agents. Agent rules and market state are sent to that provider.
- Portraits are generated by an external image model. Keys the member adds later (key vault, not built) are stored encrypted and decrypted to call the provider.
- Everything is paper trading: simulated money on real prices. Nothing can place a real order.
- Account deletion removes the member's account, sessions, agents, paper accounts, leaderboard rows and acceptance record.

## Data protection (GDPR, Data Protection Act 2018)

- **Controller**: the operator's legal name, address and contact. A DPO is probably not required at this size; counsel to confirm.
- **Purposes and lawful bases**, one by one: running the service (contract), security and abuse prevention (legitimate interest), the public leaderboard (contract, with an opt-out per agent), e-mails about the account (contract), any marketing (consent, off by default).
- **Processors and recipients**: the e-mail provider (Resend), the LLM and image provider (OpenAI), Stripe, the hosting provider. Each needs a data processing agreement.
- **International transfers**: the LLM provider and some processors are in the United States. Counsel to confirm the transfer mechanism (adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses) for each.
- **Retention**: per data type. The sign-in tokens are short-lived; leaderboard samples are kept five weeks; the acceptance record goes with the account (counsel to say whether to keep it for evidence after erasure).
- **Rights**: access, rectification, erasure, portability, restriction, objection, complaint to the Data Protection Commission. Erasure exists; a data export is designed, not built.
- **Age**: the product asks for 18 or older. Ireland's digital age of consent is 16; 18 is the safer line for a contract, counsel to confirm.
- **Cookies**: one strictly necessary session cookie, no analytics, so no consent banner is needed under the ePrivacy rules, but the notice must say so. If analytics are added this changes.

## Consumer law and payments (EU)

- Subscriptions sold to consumers: the 14-day right of withdrawal, the express request to start at once and the acknowledgement that it is lost on full use, clear price including VAT, renewal and cancellation terms, the information before purchase.
- **VAT**: Irish VAT on digital services to consumers across the EU (one-stop shop). Stripe Tax can compute it; counsel and the accountant decide the registration.
- **Stripe**: the operator's own account. Checkout and the Customer Portal keep card data out of the platform. Stripe Connect for seller payouts later needs its own terms and seller identity checks.
- **Marketplace** (later): the EU Digital Services Act terms for a platform that hosts third-party content: a notice-and-action route for reports, statement of reasons when content is removed, transparency about how skills are ranked.
- **Affiliates** (later): commission only on real purchases, mandatory disclosure that a link is an affiliate link, no income claims.

## Financial wording

- The product says in several places that the money is simulated and that nothing is advice. Counsel to confirm that the wording keeps it outside investment advice and financial promotion rules, and whether leaderboard results or shared rules need extra disclaimers.
- If live trading is ever offered, it needs its own legal review (authorisation and custody questions) before any design work.

## Where the drafts live

- `dashboard/src/ArenaLegal.tsx` renders the four pages with a draft banner. The text to replace is the `legal.*` strings in `dashboard/src/i18n/en.ts` and the five translations.
- The consent screen (`ArenaConsent.tsx`) and the consent version (`CONSENT_VERSION` in `src/arena/store.ts`) must change together: a new version asks every member again.

## Status of the texts, 2 Oct 2026

The four pages (Terms, Privacy notice, Risk notice, Cookies) now carry full draft text, in English, in `dashboard/src/legalText.ts`. It was written from what the Arena does, and `test/arena-legal.test.ts` checks every fact that can be checked against the code (retention periods, the cookie and the two stored preferences, prices, the quarantine, the minimum age, the companies that receive data, that real trading is not offered). **It has not been reviewed by a lawyer and the pages say so.** Other languages show a notice and the English text: only the English text is stated to be binding, and translating legal texts is for counsel.

To finish, the operator must:
1. Set `ARENA_OPERATOR_NAME`, `_ADDRESS`, `_COMPANY_NO`, `_VAT`, `_EMAIL`, `_PRIVACY_EMAIL`. Until they are set the pages show each missing detail as `[x missing]`.
2. Fill the bracketed points in the text, which are shown highlighted on the page: the hosting provider's name and country, the backup schedule, the payment-record retention period.
3. Have counsel go through every `[Counsel to confirm ...]` point (14-day withdrawal wording at checkout, the liability cap, the governing law and consumer protection wording, the transfer mechanism for each non-EEA provider) and the whole text, then set `ARENA_LEGAL_REVIEWED_ON=YYYY-MM-DD`. Only then does the draft banner turn into "Reviewed by counsel on ...".
4. Decide whether the product is a regulated service (see section 5 of `ARENA_AUTONOMY_LIVE.md`), which would change the Terms and the Risk notice.

Two changes were made to the product so the text is true: sign-in link records (which hold an e-mail address) are now deleted one day after they expire, expired sessions are removed, and the security log is kept 365 days (`purgeExpired`). The consent version was bumped to `2026-10-draft2`, so everyone is asked to accept the new text. There is no data-download button yet: the Privacy notice says to ask by e-mail. Decide whether to build one before launch.
